Internet Technologies: One topic I found particularly interesting was the difference between the Internet and an intranet. I learned that the Internet is a global network that provides access to publicly available resources and services, while an intranet is a private network used within an organization to provide controlled access to information and resources. The activity also helped me better understand how technologies such as IP addresses, DNS, protocols, web servers, and browsers work together to support Internet-based services. This connects directly to my experience as a Technical Director for Virtual Instructor-Led Training (VILT). When supporting live classes through platforms such as Zoom, I am working with technology that depends on Internet connectivity, even though many of the underlying processes are not visible to the instructor or participants. Understanding the distinction between public Internet resources and private organizational networks could be valuable when supporting future learning environments where some resources may be available publicly while others require access through an organization's network. Tracing Internet Traffic: Another topic I learned about was how Internet traffic travels between computers and servers. I learned that information sent over the Internet can pass through multiple routers, or hops, before reaching its destination. Traceroute tools can display these hops and provide useful information when investigating connectivity problems. This has a direct connection to my experience as a VILT Technical Director. During a live virtual class, problems with an instructor's or participant's Internet connection can affect audio, video, screen sharing, or other aspects of the session. Understanding how traffic moves across a network gives me a better conceptual foundation for understanding where connectivity problems may occur. I can apply this knowledge in future VILT or technical learning environments by better understanding network-related problems and communicating more effectively with instructors, participants, or IT personnel when troubleshooting issues. Git and Version Control: A third topic I learned about was Git and version control. I learned that Git allows developers to track changes to files and maintain a history of a project's development. This is especially relevant to my current studies in web development and my interest in technical learning and multimedia. My VILT work has involved managing live instruction and digital media, while web-based learning environments can require the creation and revision of many digital files. Git provides a way to keep track of those changes and preserve previous versions of a project. I can apply this knowledge to future work involving websites, interactive learning materials, or other digital training resources. Understanding version control also gives me a better appreciation of the development practices behind the online tools and resources used in modern learning environments.
